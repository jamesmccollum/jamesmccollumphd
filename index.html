Work with me
Therapy

Therapy is a particular kind of conversation. One where you're allowed to just follow the thread and see where it goes. In the therapeutic relationship, my job is to pay close attention to what you're really saying and to work together to find new insights that lead to meaningful personal change.

In practice that looks different for different people. Sometimes it's heavy. Sometimes it's light. Sometimes it moves slowly, and then all at once. It's often sad. It's also often funny. We humans are hilarious creatures. Crying and laughter can both be signs that something real is happening. However it looks, I want therapy to feel alive for you.

Consultation

I consult with practicing clinicians, particularly those interested in Control Mastery Theory and other post-common factors approaches. Good therapy tends to flow from a good case formulation, whether written down and explicit or implicitly understood. My focus in consultation is the movement from process (how the patient is guiding you toward what would be more helpful) to formulation, and then to ideas about intervention.

For prelicensed clinicians looking for supervision, reach out to me at the San Francisco Therapy Group.

Research

My research focuses on what actually happens inside successful therapy, and what can go wrong. At a high level, therapy is all about helping the patient get what they need. I'm particularly interested in how that happens. How patients communicate what they need to their therapists, and how therapists can best respond to their patients.

Psychotherapy is in a weird place as a field. We have pretty good evidence that psychotherapy is helpful for people, but there's a lot of disagreement on why. The best evidence says that what we've been focusing on as a field, largely specific models and techniques (all the three-letter acronyms you've heard), isn't the important part. All therapy approaches have roughly equivalent outcomes overall. Therapists who specialize in these models don't tend to get better outcomes than those who don't.

My take is that what really matters is specificity. I've picked out a few of my publications below.

About

I'm in this field because I love exploring why people do what they do. I'm a therapist. I do research. I teach, train, supervise, and consult with other therapists. It's all in service of this fascination with people and our idiosyncratic subjectivities.

I've been licensed as a California psychologist for over a decade. I cofounded the San Francisco Therapy Group and serve as vice president of the San Francisco Psychotherapy Research Group, where I also co-chair the continuing education committee.
